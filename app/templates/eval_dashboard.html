3. Key Technical Findings & Changes Made

Rigorous persona testing identified four critical edge cases, resulting in direct prompt and backend code refinements.

1. Rule R1: Single-Question Pacing on Small LLMs

Failure Observed: Small models (like those running in Claude Code or low-turn agents) frequently bundled 5 to 7 field inquiries into a single message ("Please provide your name, DOB, block, bank account, and IFSC"). Elderly applicants and kiosk operators were consistently confused and skipped fields.

Engineering Fix: Enforced Rule R1 in system prompts ("Ask ONE question per reply. Stop. Wait for citizen's answer") coupled with structured, machine-parsable next_step payloads that dictate single-field progression.

2. Early Pilot Block Fail-Fast at Eligibility Check

Failure Observed: Testing with Geeta Devi (P05) from Namti revealed that checking AGENT_BLOCKS solely at submit_application caused ineligible citizens to spend 10 minutes entering personal, address, and bank information before being turned away.

Engineering Fix: Moved pilot block validation into check_eligibility(dob, block) on Turn 2. Non-pilot residents are immediately and respectfully redirected to the block development counter before submitting sensitive data.

3. Declared-Only Document Safeguard with Mandatory Attestation

Failure Observed: Chat-based assistants cannot easily capture physical paper documents from rural citizens. Early officer MCP prototypes risked approving records where no human had ever inspected the applicant's identity proof.

Engineering Fix: Applications without file attachments are stored with doc_path = 'declared://'. In officer_mcp.py, calling decide_application(..., decision="APPROVE") fails with DECLARED_ONLY_UNCONFIRMED unless the officer explicitly passes confirmed_original_seen=True, enforcing statutory human accountability.

4. Zero-Trust OAuth 2.1 Over In-Chat OTP Collection

Failure Observed: Initial prototypes exposed send_otp and verify_otp directly as MCP tools, training citizens to share OTPs with chatbots.

Engineering Fix: Replaced in-chat OTP with the portal's native OAuth 2.1 server. Citizens authenticate on the official portal via mobile + OTP + consent; the AI client receives a scoped RS256 JWT with audience validation. The model never touches credentials.

5. Architectural Decisions

Three Decisions That Mattered Most:

  1. JWT over OTP-in-MCP: Protects citizens against credential phishing and enables cryptographic audience isolation between Citizen MCP (:8001) and Officer MCP (:8002).
  2. Pilot Block Scope Enforced at Eligibility: Prevents non-pilot block citizens from expending unnecessary effort entering bank details.
  3. Declared-Only Age Proof with Officer Confirmation: Structurally prevents AI or officers from approving applications without physical document verification.

One Decision to Make Differently With More Time:

Redis-Backed Distributed Session State: While production JWTs are stateless, ephemeral OTP rate limits and temporary staging currently run on local storage / PostgreSQL. With more time, Redis would back transient session counters to support horizontal scaling across multi-region server clusters.
